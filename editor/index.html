LUFS is the loudness over the last 3 seconds. Peak L and R show the left and right peaks in dB. Comp and Limit show how many dB the compressor and limiter take away.

The compressor and limiter add back some of the level they take (Web Audio's makeup gain), so a lower threshold can make the mix louder. Watch the loudness meter.

    Read the AI instructions (Markdown)

    A musicbox score is a plain-text .scrim file. It names instruments, gives them notes to play, and arranges the music into cues. Every sound is synthesized in your browser.

    A small score

    This loops two bars. Try it in the editor, then change a note or the tempo.

    title Shore
tempo 96
beats 4
steps 4
key A minor

voice tune flute
voice bass fretless

pattern A
harmony | Am | F |
tune    | A4:8 C5:8 | F5:8 A5:8 |
bass    | A1:16 | F1:16 |

cue explore
loop A

    How a score fits together

    tempo 96
    Beats per minute. Higher is faster.
    beats 4, steps 4
    Four beats per bar, divided into four steps each: 16 steps in every bar.
    voice tune flute
    A part called tune, played by a flute. Other instruments include piano, harp, strings, celesta and drums.
    pattern A
    A named passage. Each pair of bar lines contains one bar of notes for a voice.
    harmony | Am | F |
    The chord in each bar. It guides the checker and parts that follow the harmony.
    loop A
    Repeat A. Use play intro before it for a passage that plays once.

    Notes, rests and chords

    Every bar must add up to its number of steps. At 4 beats × 4 steps, that is 16.

    C5
    A C in octave 5, lasting one step. Sharps and flats work too: F#4, Bb3.
    C5:8 or C5-------
    Hold C for eight steps. Use :N to make long notes easy to count.
    .:4
    Four steps of silence. A dot alone is one step.
    [Am]:16
    A whole bar of A minor. [*]:16 follows the harmony line.
    %, _, =
    Used alone in a bar: repeat the previous bar, rest for a bar, or hold the previous note through it.
    x... x... x... x...
    A drum bar. Each character is a step: x hits, X accents, o is quiet, and . rests.

    Cues and intensity

    A cue is music for a game state, such as explore or combat. Each cue has its own play and loop lines. The cue buttons let you hear how they hand over.

    Add layer=1 or layer=2 to a voice to bring it in at that intensity. Layer 0 is always present. The intensity slider adds and removes these parts while the music keeps its place.

    switch explore -> combat at bar fade 1 waits for a bar boundary and fades the old cue for a second. A via pattern can add a fill between them.

    Music that changes each time

    {A4 C5 E5} picks a note each time it plays. {chord} picks from the current chord. A voice's chance=0.7 plays each note about 70% of the time, and arp=updown turns chords into moving notes.

    loop A {B C} chooses B or C on each pass. Continuous beds such as bed sea sea vol=0.5 add ambience that carries across cue changes.

    Editing and listening

    Press Ctrl+Enter to apply score edits. Problems points to lines that need fixing; Analysis flags notes to review. The Master controls change the sound immediately and write their settings into the score.
